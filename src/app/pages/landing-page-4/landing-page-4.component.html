THE MYSTERY

Golf is a game of details. Across the country every weekend golfers make this mistake every time they tee up the ball. The crazy part is its right in front of their eyes and they don't even know it. In fact, most golfers blame their swing for their errant drives when the real culprit is their clubface alignment.

The face angle at impact is the #1 factor of ball flight and distance, yet it's the one thing most golfers have no idea how to check or adjust. Aiming your driver face just 1 tick left or right on a clock face at impact is the difference between a perfect drive and a ball lost in the woods. The mistake is repeated over and over regardless of how "shallow" or "good" a player's swing is because they think their club face is straight when it's not.

1 minute on a clock = 6° = Huge Miss

The Problem

The Slice and Hook Trap

Drivers are really hard to aim. Driver heads have no flat edges, grooves, or aiming guides making aiming the clubface a complete guessing game. To make matters worse your eyes are positioned really far behind the club head making it near impossible without a lot of trial and error. I like to compare it to trying to level a picture frame that is an oval instead of a square.

  • 1
    Drivers Are Hard to Aim

    To maximize distance and aerodynamics club manufactures make driver heads very complex shapes. This is great on paper but in reality it causes most golfers to aim and hold their club incorrectly.

  • 2
    Small Miss, Big Trouble

    Don't overestimate your ability to aim. Just 6 degrees or minut on a clock face is enought to send your ball off the golf course costing you a stroke.

The Solution

Face Pointers

Face pointer have been around forever and simply use magnets to attach to the club face. You then use the pointer as a visual guide to make sure your face is square to the target line at impact.

  • 1
    Magnets Don't Work for Drivers

    Today's high-performance drivers are made of titanium and carbon fiber which are not magnetic. So the traditional magnetic face pointers fail to stick!

  • 2
    A Tool Is Just a Tool Without Instruction

    Just because a face pointer is on your club doesn't mean everything is fixed. Finding online resources feels like a scavenger hunt with conflicting advice and no clear path. You need a step-by-step system to learn how to use the tool and adjust your aim for real improvement.
